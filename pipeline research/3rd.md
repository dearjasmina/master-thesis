# Final Architecture & Training Recommendation: A Deterministic G-Buffer Neural Renderer for Cycles-Quality CT Organ Visualization on 4× L4

## TL;DR
- **Build a deterministic, geometry-buffer-conditioned regression renderer (a pix2pixHD/HRNet-style generator trained with L1 + LPIPS + feature-matching + a small PatchGAN, conditioned on depth + camera-space normals + segmentation-ID + flat EEVEE RGB), NOT a diffusion model.** This directly satisfies all four hard requirements: cross-view consistency comes essentially "for free" because a deterministic function of view-consistent inputs produces view-consistent outputs, and it maximizes your primary metric (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS fidelity to the Cycles ground truth).
- **Analysis A is closer to correct than Analysis B for your constraints, but its diffusion-first ranking should be inverted.** Analysis B's heavyweight multi-view machinery (joint multi-view diffusion, MV-Adapter cross-view attention over 20 views, RL/Carve3D, 3DGS-in-the-loop) is neither necessary (3DGS is out of scope; your lighting is fixed and known) nor comfortably trainable on 4× L4 (24 GB, no NVLink, 300 GB/s). Skip all of it for the thesis.
- **4× L4 is an inference fleet, not a training cluster — and that is exactly why the GAN/regression path wins.** pix2pixHD/SPADE-class models train comfortably at 1024² in 24 GB and DDP-scale at ~90% efficiency over 4 PCIe GPUs; the full SDXL 2.6-billion-parameter UNet sits at the memory edge even on a faster RTX 4090 and is bandwidth-throttled on L4. The deterministic renderer is the only option that is simultaneously consistent-by-construction, fidelity-optimal, and trainable on your hardware.

## Key Findings

**1. Your problem is "learning the Cycles forward-rendering function," which is a regression problem, not a generation problem.** The lighting rig is fixed and known, the geometry is exact, and the input/output pairs are perfectly registered. The network's only job is to learn a deterministic shading/BRDF+SSS+GI function of (geometry buffers, fixed light rig). This is precisely the setting of *deferred neural shading* / *screen-space neural rendering* (Nalbach et al. "Deep Shading"; Thomas & Forbes "Deep Illumination"; the physics-based neural deferred shader of arXiv 2504.12273), where a CNN learns to reproduce path-traced global illumination effects from G-buffers. Intel's *Enhancing Photorealism Enhancement* (Richter, AbuAlHaija, Koltun, 2021) is the canonical architecture: a G-buffer-encoder feeding an image-enhancement network, trained with **LPIPS (to retain structure) + a perceptual discriminator (for realism)** — explicitly designed to "eliminate artifacts ... which often hallucinate objects."

**2. Diffusion models structurally trade away your primary metric and introduce your forbidden failure mode.** The perception–distortion tradeoff (Blau & Michaeli) is a proven theorem: you cannot simultaneously maximize perceptual realism and pixel fidelity. Multiple surveys confirm "models trained with pixel-based loss tend to achieve high PSNR, whereas generative [diffusion] models tend to produce lower PSNR." Since your success metric is **primarily fidelity to the held-out Cycles GT (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS)**, a diffusion model is optimizing the wrong objective. Worse, diffusion sampling is stochastic, and in medical imaging this manifests as exactly the failures your advisor will reject: hallucinated structures and replicated noise.

**3. The single most decisive piece of evidence: per-view diffusion G-buffer renderers empirically fail cross-view consistency — exactly Analysis B's objection, but it indicts diffusion, not single-view generation per se.** FrameDiffuser (arXiv 2512.16670, Dec 2025) directly tested the RGB↔X (Zeng et al., SIGGRAPH 2024) per-image G-buffer→RGB diffusion renderer across views and found "**severe temporal inconsistencies: lighting changes drastically between Frame 1 and Frame 3, with door frame shadows appearing and disappearing, and floor lighting patterns shifting completely.**" It also notes RGB↔X output "appear[s] artificially flat ... lacking the rich lighting variation." This is precisely the drift Analysis B feared. But the lesson is the *opposite* of Analysis B's recommendation: the cause is **per-view stochastic generation**, not the lack of cross-view attention. A *deterministic* generator on identical view-consistent inputs cannot exhibit this drift.

**4. The medical-imaging literature has independently converged on "regression, not generation."** "Regression is all you need for medical image translation" (Rassmann, Kügler, Ewert & Reuter, arXiv 2505.02048, IEEE TMI 2025 — the YODA paper) shows across five diverse multimodal datasets that "regression sampling is not only substantially more efficient but also matches or exceeds image quality of full diffusion sampling," and warns that GAN/DM "core strengths — creativity and realism — can be detrimental in medical applications, where accuracy and fidelity are paramount ... [they] risk introducing hallucinations and replication of unwanted acquisition noise." Cohen et al. ("Distribution Matching Losses Can Hallucinate Features in Medical Image Translation") established that adversarial distribution-matching can invent or erase features — which is why we keep the adversarial term *small and patch-local* (a sharpness regularizer), with L1+LPIPS dominating.

**5. Geometry-buffer conditioning is the right input representation, and it is the load-bearing consistency mechanism.** ControlNet's own paper notes training is "robust with small (<50k) datasets," and EPE/DiffusionRenderer/RGB↔X/FrameDiffuser all confirm G-buffers (depth, normals, segmentation, material) are the standard conditioning for neural rendering. Because Blender exports these for free and they are **view-consistent by construction** (depth/normals/seg-ID are exact functions of geometry+pose), conditioning on them means the generator's input is already multi-view-consistent. A deterministic map then yields multi-view-consistent output.

## Details

### Adjudication: Analysis A vs Analysis B against your clarified constraints

**Where Analysis B is wrong for you.** Analysis B's central claim — "per-view 2D models cannot guarantee multi-view consistency because they generate each view independently with no cross-view mechanism; slight randomness causes drift" — is correct *only for stochastic generators in unknown-lighting / in-the-wild settings*. Its entire argument is premised on (a) randomness per view and (b) unknown lighting/geometry that the network must hallucinate consistently. **Both premises are false in your setup.** Your lighting is a fixed 4-light rig, identical across all views; your geometry is exact and exported as buffers; and a GAN/regression generator is *deterministic given its input*. The "slight randomness → drift → floaters" causal chain has no randomness to start it. Analysis B is essentially solving the MVDream/SyncDreamer problem (generate plausible novel views of an under-determined object), which is a fundamentally harder, ill-posed problem than yours (reproduce a known deterministic render). Your problem is well-posed and fully supervised.

Furthermore, Analysis B's recommended stack is **not trainable on your hardware**. MV-Adapter achieves multi-view generation at only 768 resolution on SDXL and is built around processing a small fixed set of views (e.g., 6) jointly; cross-view attention over **20 views at 1024²** means the attention sequence length scales with ~20× the tokens, which is infeasible in 24 GB without NVLink — before even considering that full SDXL fine-tuning is already at the 24 GB edge on a faster 4090. RL fine-tuning (Carve3D) and 3DGS-in-the-loop (MV2MV) require differentiable 3D reconstruction inside the training loop — multi-GPU, high-memory, and pointless given 3DGS is explicitly out of scope. Analysis B is an 8×A100 research program, not a master's thesis on 4× L4.

**Where Analysis A is right, and where to adjust it.** Analysis A correctly identified (a) multi-buffer G-buffer conditioning, (b) determinism (fixed seed/noise + LPIPS + feature matching), and (c) Pix2PixHD/SPADE as the consistent-by-construction fallback. The adjustment: **promote the fallback to the primary.** Analysis A's diffusion-first ranking inverts the right priority for a fidelity metric. A fine-tuned SDXL ControlNet, even with a fixed seed, still (i) optimizes perceptual realism over PSNR, (ii) inherits a VAE that lossily round-trips fine detail (capping achievable PSNR/SSIM), and (iii) carries residual hallucination risk that your advisor will reject. The deterministic generator has none of these liabilities and is strictly better aligned with your metric. Keep diffusion only as a documented "realism-stretch" comparison if reviewers ask for maximum texture realism and you can demonstrate it does not hallucinate.

### The key technical question: how consistent is a deterministic G-buffer generator across views?

**Argument that it is inherently view-consistent.** Let the generator be a deterministic function f. Its inputs for view i are (depth_i, normals_i, segID_i, flatRGB_i), all of which are exact, noise-free renders of the *same* geometry under the *same* fixed lights from pose i. For two views i and j that observe the same surface patch P, the *local* shading of P is a function of P's geometry and the fixed lights; the buffers encode that geometry identically (up to projection), so f produces the same appearance for P in both views — modulo legitimate view-dependent effects (specular lobes, Fresnel) that *should* differ and that the camera-space normals / view direction encode. There is no per-view latent noise to cause arbitrary divergence. Contrast a diffusion model: even with a fixed seed, the initial latent is tied to a per-view coordinate frame, and the iterative denoiser amplifies tiny input differences into different texture/lighting realizations — the FrameDiffuser failure. **A GAN/regression generator is consistent by construction; a diffusion model is consistent only approximately and only with fragile tricks.** This directly undercuts Analysis B's core objection, which was framed for unknown-lighting scenarios.

**The residual risk and the cheap fix.** The one place a deterministic generator can still drift is high-frequency *texture* synthesized partly by the adversarial term (the discriminator can paint slightly different micro-texture in regions where the input buffers are locally ambiguous, e.g., large smooth organ surfaces). Because your camera poses and depth maps are known *exactly*, you can add a near-free **multi-view reprojection consistency loss**: warp the generated view i into view j's frame using the known relative pose and depth_i (a fixed-parameter spatial-transformer warp, as in PlaneRCNN / self-supervised-depth literature — no epipolar estimation needed because poses are known), mask occlusions from depth, and penalize the photometric/feature difference in co-visible pixels. This is the cheapest possible consistency mechanism and is far lighter than cross-view attention.

### GAN vs Diffusion vs pure regression for THIS case

| Criterion | Deterministic regression+GAN (recommended) | Fine-tuned SDXL ControlNet (diffusion) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary metric (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS to GT) | **Best** — L1/LPIPS directly optimize it | Worse PSNR/SSIM by design (perception–distortion tradeoff; VAE round-trip) |
| Cross-view consistency | **Consistent by construction** (deterministic on consistent inputs) | Stochastic drift documented (FrameDiffuser) |
| Hallucination / invented anatomy | Low (L1+LPIPS dominate; small patch-GAN) | Higher (generative prior, documented in medical MIT) |
| Trains at 1024² in 24 GB | **Yes, comfortably** | At the memory edge even on faster 4090 |
| DDP on 4× L4 (no NVLink) | **~90% efficiency** (ResNet/U-Net regime) | 2.6 B-param UNet approaches comm-bound regime |
| Texture realism ceiling | High with LPIPS+GAN | Marginally higher, but at fidelity/consistency cost |

The pix2pix ablations (Isola et al.) are instructive: pure L1 gives "reasonable but blurry" results (it regresses to the conditional mean and produces "average, grayish colors"); adding a **70×70 PatchGAN** restores sharp, correct high-frequency texture without the instability of a global image GAN. The EPE recipe (LPIPS + perceptual discriminator + G-buffer encoder) is the field-tested instantiation for exactly "render-to-photoreal" translation. So: **regression backbone + LPIPS + small PatchGAN**, not a pure regressor (too blurry) and not a diffusion model (wrong objective, drift, hallucination).

### Trainability on 4× L4 — concrete verdict

- **L4 spec reality:** 24 GB GDDR6, **300 GB/s** bandwidth, PCIe Gen4 x16, **no NVLink**, 72 W TDP (NVIDIA L4 Product Brief PB-11316-001, March 2023: "24 GB of GDDR6 memory, x16 PCIe Gen4 connectivity at a 72 W maximum power envelope"). Jarvis Labs' 2026 L4 guide states "300 GB/s memory bandwidth is 6.7x lower than the A100's 2 TB/s... No NVLink, no MIG," and bluntly: "**Not designed for training. While you can train small models on an L4, the limited VRAM and bandwidth make it impractical for anything beyond experimental fine-tuning of 7B models.**" There are essentially **no public wall-clock L4 training benchmarks** for SD/ControlNet/GANs — a caveat to flag in the thesis.
- **pix2pixHD/SPADE at 1024²: comfortable.** The official NVIDIA/pix2pixHD README states: "To train the images at full resolution (2048 x 1024) requires a GPU with 24G memory ... or 16G memory if using mixed precision (AMP)." Your target is 1024×1024 — *half* the pixels of 2048×1024 — so it fits well within 24 GB (~12–16 GB range), leaving headroom for extra input channels and the discriminator. The same repo supports raw RGB+buffer input via `--label_nc 0`. Wall-clock references: pix2pixHD at 960×512 trained 20 epochs in ~1 day on a single GPU; ~15 min/epoch for ~2,250 images on an A100. On an L4 expect roughly 2.5–4× slower per step than a 4090 (bandwidth/power-bound), but with DDP across 4 cards this is very manageable for ~28.5k pairs.
- **DDP without NVLink: strong for this model class.** The official PyTorch DDP paper (Li et al., VLDB 2020) shows ResNet-50-class models are *not* bandwidth-bottlenecked ("for relatively small models like ResNet50, bandwidth is not the bottleneck"); large transformers (BERT+) are. A U-Net/pix2pixHD-class generator (tens to a few hundred M params) sits firmly in the ResNet regime. Measured 4-GPU DDP efficiency for ResNet/U-Net-class models is **~90% (per-GPU scale factor 0.90–0.95)** even on PCIe RTX 4090/A5000 (AIME benchmarks), and PCIe-vs-NVLink differences are small for ResNet-style workloads below 8 GPUs (Warwick PMBS interconnect study). Raise `bucket_cap_mb` to reduce all-reduce frequency. **Verdict: 4× L4 DDP is well-suited to the deterministic renderer.**
- **SDXL ControlNet at 1024² on L4: possible but painful.** The SDXL UNet is **2.6 B parameters** (Podell et al., arXiv 2307.01952). Full SDXL fine-tune fits 24 GB only with 8-bit Adam + gradient checkpointing + precomputed embeddings + batch 1, and is bandwidth-throttled on L4; the 2.6 B-param all-reduce over PCIe pushes toward the comm-bound regime. Training a full ControlNet on SD1.5 (≈865 M-param UNet) on a single 24 GB Ada card is documented (batch 12–64 with accumulation), so an **SD1.5-ControlNet** is the only diffusion variant I would consider feasible here — and even that is a stretch experiment, not the primary.
- **MV-Adapter / cross-view attention over 20 views at 1024²: not feasible.** MV-Adapter tops out at 768 resolution on SDXL for a handful of views; 20-view joint attention at 1024² in 24 GB without NVLink is out of reach. **Verdict: infeasible; skip.**

### The neural-renderer framing favors determinism

Framed as "learn the Cycles forward-rendering function," the closest prior art is deterministic and G-buffer-conditioned: Deep Shading, Deep Illumination (cGAN), Intel EPE, and the physics-based neural deferred shader (arXiv 2504.12273), which "renders an arbitrary G-buffer ... under a given light, producing a photo-realistic image" trained by "minimizing the difference between rendered and ground truth images" — i.e., supervised regression. Even NVIDIA's DiffusionRenderer (CVPR 2025), though diffusion-based, is explicitly "a neural approximation of path-traced shading ... designed to remain faithful to the conditioning signals," and it needed video-diffusion priors and massive data specifically because it targets *unknown real-world* scenes ("bypass the need for precise scene representations"). Your scenes are *known and synthetic*, which removes the need for a generative prior entirely. The framing strongly favors a deterministic G-buffer renderer.

## Recommendations

### PRIMARY ARCHITECTURE (Stage 1 — build this first)

**Input representation (9–12 channels):**
- **Flat EEVEE RGB** (3 ch) — your original input, keeps per-organ diffuse color.
- **Depth** (1 ch): export linear Z, normalize per-volume to [0,1] using the fixed near/far of the orbit (constant across the 20 views so the encoding is view-consistent). Store 16-bit to preserve precision.
- **Surface normals** (3 ch): export in **camera space** (view space), not world space. Camera-space normals let the network learn view-dependent shading (specular/Fresnel) correctly; re-derive per view. (Optionally *also* provide world-space normals as 3 extra channels if VRAM allows — world-space normals are identical for a surface point across views and reinforce consistency; a cheap ablation.)
- **Segmentation-ID** (1–3 ch): encode the per-organ ID. Best practice: a learned embedding table mapping each of the 117 TotalSegmentator v2 classes to a small vector, fed via SPADE-style spatially-adaptive normalization (so organ identity modulates the BRDF/SSS the network applies). Simpler fallback: the per-organ diffuse hex color already *is* a segmentation encoding, but an explicit ID map avoids color collisions.
- Optionally an **ambient-occlusion** buffer if Blender exports it cheaply.

**Backbone:** A high-resolution encoder–decoder. Two good choices, in order:
1. **pix2pixHD generator** (coarse-to-fine global+local generator, ngf 32) with a **multi-scale PatchGAN discriminator** and **feature-matching loss** — proven at 1024p in 24 GB, off-the-shelf NVIDIA repo, `--label_nc 0` for RGB+buffer concatenation, easy to extend input channels.
2. **SPADE/GauGAN generator** if you want segmentation-ID to drive spatially-adaptive normalization explicitly (strong for "organ identity → material"). Note SPADE's official config is heavier (8×V100 in the paper) but trains at lower res/batch on 24 GB.

Use pix2pixHD as the default; treat SPADE as an ablation.

**Resolution strategy:** Train **natively at 1024²**. pix2pixHD fits 1024² in well under 24 GB. Do NOT train at 512 + upsample as the primary — your fidelity metric rewards native high-frequency detail and you have the VRAM. (A 512 prototype is fine for fast iteration in week 1.)

**Loss function (deterministic generator):**
- **L1** (pixel) — weight 10. Anchors fidelity/PSNR, prevents hallucination.
- **LPIPS (or VGG perceptual)** — weight 1. Restores perceptual texture; the EPE/super-resolution-proven structure-preserving term.
- **Discriminator feature-matching** — weight 10 (pix2pixHD default). Stabilizes adversarial training, improves fidelity.
- **PatchGAN adversarial (70×70, multi-scale)** — weight 1. Sharpness only; kept small so it cannot dominate and hallucinate.
- **Multi-view reprojection consistency** — weight 1–5, **introduced in Stage 2 only if needed** (see staged plan). Warp generated view i → view j via known pose + depth_i, mask occlusions, penalize L1 + LPIPS on co-visible pixels.

**Training config (4× L4, DDP):**
- Precision: **BF16** mixed precision (Ada supports it; avoids FP16 overflow).
- Per-GPU batch size: 1–2 at 1024² (with gradient checkpointing if needed); **effective batch 8–16** via gradient accumulation × 4 GPUs.
- Optimizer: Adam, lr 2e-4 (pix2pixHD default), β=(0.5, 0.999); TTUR optional.
- DDP: NCCL backend, `bucket_cap_mb` raised (e.g., 50) to reduce PCIe all-reduce frequency; expect ~90% scaling.
- Schedule: ~100–200 epochs over ~28.5k pairs (pix2pixHD converged in tens of epochs on Cityscapes-scale data). Expect days, not weeks, on 4× L4.
- xFormers/flash-attention only relevant if you add attention; the CNN backbone does not need it.

**Train/val/test split BY VOLUME, not by view:** Split the CT volumes (note discrepancy below) at the **volume level**, e.g. 85/7.5/7.5%. All 20 views of a held-out volume go entirely to val or test. This prevents the trivial leakage of seeing 19 views of an organ at train and testing on the 20th. Report metrics on held-out *volumes*.

**Paired-identical augmentation:** Apply the **exact same geometric transform** (random crop, flip, scale) to RGB input, all G-buffers, AND the Cycles GT simultaneously. **Never** apply photometric augmentation (color jitter, brightness, contrast) to the GT — that would corrupt the fidelity target and teach the net to hallucinate color. If you flip horizontally, you must also negate the x-component of camera-space normals. Prefer minimal augmentation (the dataset is already large and perfectly registered); over-augmenting can hurt a fidelity task.

### Evaluation protocol (matches your metric)

1. **Per-image fidelity on held-out volumes:** PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS vs Cycles GT. Primary ranking metric. Report mean ± std across all test views.
2. **Cross-view consistency check (the advisor's gate):** For each test volume, take view pairs (i, j) with overlap; warp generated_i into frame j using known pose + depth_i; compute masked photometric error and a deep-feature (LPIPS) error on co-visible pixels. Report this **against the same metric computed on the Cycles GT pairs** — the GT is itself not perfectly reprojection-consistent (genuine view-dependent specular/Fresnel effects), so the GT value is your floor/reference. If generated cross-view error ≈ GT cross-view error, you have passed.
3. **Anatomy / no-hallucination check:** (a) Run TotalSegmentator (or compare against the known seg-ID map) on generated images to confirm organ boundaries match input geometry — penalize any region where generated organ extent diverges from the seg-ID map. (b) Expert/advisor visual review of a fixed sample for invented structures. (c) Optionally a hallucination-index / uncertainty map.

### Staged plan with go/no-go thresholds

- **Stage 0 (week 1):** 512² pix2pixHD prototype on a ~100-volume subset, RGB+depth+normals+segID. Goal: pipeline works end-to-end, loss decreases, qualitative sanity. *Go criterion:* outputs visibly photorealistic, no obvious anatomy distortion.
- **Stage 1 (the deliverable):** Full 1024² deterministic pix2pixHD with L1+LPIPS+FM+PatchGAN on all volumes, DDP on 4× L4. Evaluate with the full protocol. *Go criterion to declare success:* held-out-volume LPIPS competitive **and cross-view reprojection error within ~10–20% of the GT-pair reference**. If the consistency check passes here (it should, by construction), **you are done — do not add complexity.**
- **Stage 2 (only IF Stage 1's cross-view check fails):** Add the **known-pose reprojection consistency loss** (weight 1–5) and/or world-space normal channels. Re-evaluate. This is cheap and almost always sufficient to close any residual texture drift.
- **Stage 3 (only IF Stage 2 still fails AND reviewers demand more texture realism):** Train an **SD1.5-ControlNet** variant on the same buffers with a **fixed per-volume seed**, as a documented realism-vs-fidelity comparison — explicitly measuring whether it hallucinates. Treat as a research aside, not the main result.
- **Reach (explicitly beyond thesis):** 3DGS / Splatfacto-W reconstruction from the consistent renders. Out of scope per your own downgrade.

### What to SKIP from Analysis B, and the failure conditions that would change that

- **MV-Adapter / cross-view attention over 20 views:** SKIP. Not trainable at 1024² in 24 GB without NVLink, and unnecessary because deterministic conditioning already gives consistency. *Reconsider only if:* a deterministic generator + reprojection loss still shows view-dependent texture drift that the reprojection loss cannot fix (very unlikely given fixed lighting) — and even then, prefer the cheap reprojection loss first.
- **Joint multi-view diffusion (MVDream/SyncDreamer):** SKIP. Solves an ill-posed novel-view problem you don't have (your views are fully determined and supervised).
- **RL fine-tuning (Carve3D):** SKIP. Requires a NeRF/3DGS consistency reward in-loop; 3DGS is out of scope and this is multi-GPU-cluster-scale.
- **3DGS-reconstruction-in-the-loop (MV2MV):** SKIP. Differentiable 3DGS in the training loop is infeasible on 4× L4 and targets a downstream goal you've deprioritized.
- **Full SDXL ControlNet as primary:** SKIP as primary (memory edge + wrong objective + drift). Keep only as the Stage-3 SD1.5 realism experiment.

## Caveats
- **Volume count discrepancy:** You state 1,429 CT volumes; the public **TotalSegmentator v2 dataset is 1,228 subjects with 117 anatomical structures** (Wasserthal et al.; Zenodo record 10047292; v1 was 1,204 cases / 104 structures). Confirm your actual render count and dataset version — it affects split sizes but not the architecture choice.
- **No public L4 training benchmarks exist:** Vendors position L4 strictly for inference; the throughput estimates here are extrapolated from RTX 4090/A100 (same Ada/Ampere families) and spec ratios (L4 ≈ 2.5–4× slower per step than a 4090 for this workload, bandwidth/power-bound). Budget conservatively and benchmark your first epoch before committing to a full schedule.
- **The adversarial term is a double-edged sword:** Even a small PatchGAN can, in principle, hallucinate micro-texture. Keep its weight low relative to L1+LPIPS, and rely on the no-hallucination check to catch it. If the advisor is maximally strict, you can drop the GAN term entirely and accept slightly softer texture (pure L1+LPIPS regression) — the YODA result suggests this is often competitive, since "regression sampling ... matches or exceeds image quality of full diffusion sampling."
- **Specular/Fresnel view-dependence is legitimate and desirable:** Do not over-penalize cross-view differences — genuine view-dependent highlights *should* differ between views. That is why the consistency check is benchmarked against the GT-pair reference, not against zero.
- **VAE/latent-diffusion fidelity ceiling:** If you run the Stage-3 diffusion comparison, remember the SD VAE lossily round-trips fine detail, structurally capping PSNR/SSIM independent of the denoiser — reinforcing why the deterministic renderer is the primary.
- **AgX tonemapping / chromatic aberration / fog glow in the GT:** These are global, deterministic post-effects baked into your Cycles GT. A deterministic renderer learns them as part of the function; a stochastic model may apply them inconsistently across views. This further favors the deterministic choice.

### Bottom line
For your clarified goal (a fast neural renderer reproducing fixed-lighting, fixed-geometry Cycles output with maximum fidelity-to-GT and zero hallucinated/inconsistent lighting), on your hardware (4× L4, 24 GB, no NVLink), the correct, decisive choice is a **deterministic pix2pixHD-style G-buffer renderer (L1 + LPIPS + feature-matching + small PatchGAN)**, evaluated with held-out-volume PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS plus a known-pose reprojection consistency check. Analysis A's *fallback* is your *primary*; Analysis B's multi-view/RL/3DGS apparatus should be cut entirely. Add the cheap reprojection-consistency loss only if the cross-view gate fails, and consider an SD1.5-ControlNet only as a realism-comparison aside.

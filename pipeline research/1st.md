# Recommended Architecture for Flat Label-Map → Photorealistic Translation Feeding 3D Gaussian Splatting

## TL;DR
- **Primary recommendation: Fine-tune an SDXL ControlNet conditioned on multiple Blender-exported geometry buffers (depth + surface normals + segmentation-ID map + the flat RGB), NOT on the flat RGB alone.** Geometry conditioning is the single biggest lever for cross-view consistency, because depth/normal/ID buffers are inherently view-consistent (they are exact projections of one fixed 3D mesh), while a stochastic 2D translator left to "invent" lighting/texture per view is the primary cause of 3DGS floaters.
- **Enforce multi-view consistency with a layered defense, not one trick:** (1) condition on consistent geometry buffers; (2) fix the diffusion seed/initial latent noise per volume and add LPIPS + feature-matching losses; (3) absorb any residual photometric drift downstream with per-image appearance embeddings in the splatting stage (Splatfacto-W / WildGaussians style). This is more robust for 3DGS than any single consistency loss.
- **Strong fallback: Pix2PixHD or SPADE/GauGAN conditioned on the same buffers.** A conditional GAN is deterministic given its input, trains stably on ~28.5k pairs, runs at 1024², and side-steps diffusion's per-view stochasticity — the cleanest baseline for a thesis and the most consistent by construction, at some cost in photorealistic fidelity (subsurface-scattering "wetness") versus SDXL.

## Key Findings

1. **Your dataset (~28.5k pairs) is squarely in ControlNet's sweet spot.** The ControlNet authors (Zhang, Rao & Agrawala, "Adding Conditional Control to Text-to-Image Diffusion Models," arXiv:2302.05543, ICCV 2023) state verbatim: *"We show that the training of ControlNets is robust with small (<50k) and large (>1m) datasets."* They further note training a ControlNet "is as fast as fine-tuning a diffusion model, and the model can be trained on a personal device," and for sub-50k datasets it "can be trained to produce robust results even on desktop-grade personal GPUs." 28,580 paired images is ample for a ControlNet branch but small for full base-model fine-tuning.

2. **Diffusion stochasticity is the enemy of 3DGS — and this is the user's named risk, confirmed.** Independent per-view 2D translation with a diffusion model produces view-to-view variation that 3DGS cannot reconcile, manifesting as floaters and blur. This is documented repeatedly in 2024–2025 work: GaMO (arXiv:2512.25073) reports adapted multi-view diffusion models "suffer from multi-view inconsistency, resulting in noisy reconstructions"; Luminance-GS (arXiv:2504.01503) finds image restoration/enhancement modules "easily lead to multi-view inconsistency, which ultimately causes floaters during rendering"; Gaussian-DK (CGF 2024) shows brightness/multi-view inconsistency "poses great challenges to 3D Gaussian Splatting and severely degrades its performance."

3. **Geometry buffers are view-consistent by construction.** Depth, normals, and segmentation-ID maps are deterministic projections of one fixed mesh, so conditioning on them ties every view to the same underlying 3D signal. ControlNet natively supports depth, normal (Bae's method), and segmentation conditioning, and supports composing multiple conditions: per the diffusers docs and the original paper, when multiple ControlNets are used "the outputs from each ControlNet are added together to create one combined additional conditioning" — no extra weighting needed.

4. **The closest published analogs to this exact task all condition on full G-buffers.** Intel's *Enhancing Photorealism Enhancement* (Richter, Abu AlHaija & Koltun, IEEE TPAMI vol. 45 pp. 1700–1715, DOI 10.1109/TPAMI.2022.3166687; arXiv:2105.04619) translates synthetic GTA renders to photoreal using G-buffers — *normal, depth, albedo, glossiness, atmosphere, segmentation* — injected via Rendering-Aware Denormalization (RAD) blocks in an HRNet, trained with an LPIPS loss plus a perceptual discriminator; the G-buffers are credited with the method's strong temporal and semantic stability. NVIDIA's **DiffusionRenderer** (Liang et al., CVPR 2025 Oral; arXiv:2501.18590) does forward neural rendering from G-buffers `{n, d, a, r, m, E}` = normals, depth, base color/albedo, roughness, metallic, plus environment-map lighting, on a **Stable Video Diffusion** backbone at 512×512; its project page states the forward renderer *"generates accurate shadows and reflections that are consistent across viewpoints... synthesized entirely from an environment map, despite the input G-buffers containing no explicit shadow or reflection information."* **FrameDiffuser** (arXiv:2512.16670) conditions **SD 1.5 + ControlNet** on a 10-channel input — 9 G-buffer channels (basecolor 3, normals 3, depth 1, roughness 1, metallic 1) plus 1 computed irradiance channel — adding a ControlLoRA (rank-64) on the previous frame's latent for temporal coherence.

5. **Multi-view/video diffusion that attends across views jointly is the highest-consistency generative option, but heavier.** DiffusionRenderer's video (SVD) backbone uses temporal attention across up to 24 frames to yield multi-frame coherent shadows; it injects lighting through repurposed cross-attention layers, was trained for 20,000 iterations at 512×512 on 32 A100s over ~2 days using 150,000+ synthetic videos plus auto-labeled DL3DV-10K real video, and runs ~20 s per 24-frame clip on an A100. Zero123++ (arXiv:2310.15110) generates 3D-consistent multi-view sets by tiling six views into one image and sharing attention, "overcoming common issues like texture degradation and geometric misalignment." These inherently consistent approaches are more robust than per-view + consistency loss, but require more engineering, more VRAM, and (for the multi-view-NVS family) are not built for paired supervised translation.

6. **Downstream appearance embeddings are a proven safety net.** Splatfacto-W (Xu, Kerr et al., arXiv:2407.12306) integrates per-Gaussian neural color features and per-image appearance embeddings into 3DGS rasterization; verbatim: *"Our method improves the Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) by an average of 5.3 dB compared to 3DGS, enhances training speed by 150 times compared to NeRF-based methods."* WildGaussians does similarly. The latent appearance code is continuous, so it "captures variation in appearance without affecting 3D geometry" — exactly the property needed to absorb residual per-view photometric drift without corrupting the reconstruction.

## Details

### Architecture comparison

**Pix2Pix / Pix2PixHD (conditional GAN).** Pix2PixHD (Wang, Liu, Zhu, Tao, Kautz & Catanzaro, CVPR 2018) uses a coarse-to-fine generator — a global generator G1 outputting at 1024×512 and a local enhancer G2 outputting 2048×1024 — with three multi-scale discriminators (D1, D2, D3) and a discriminator feature-matching loss (λ=10). It is paired-supervised and, critically, **deterministic given its input**: the same conditioning always maps to the same output, which is precisely what cross-view consistency wants. SPADE/GauGAN (Park et al., NVlabs) improves on it for label-map→photo by injecting the semantic layout through spatially-adaptive (de)normalization rather than letting conventional normalization "wash away" semantic information; for a per-organ label-map input this is the most natural GAN architecture. Weakness: GANs can train unstably (mode collapse) and typically produce lower-fidelity, less convincing subsurface/wet-specular realism than a modern diffusion prior.

**ControlNet + SDXL (primary).** Freezes a pretrained text-to-image backbone and trains a parallel control branch via zero-initialized convolutions; robust on small datasets and preserves a strong photoreal prior. Supports multi-conditioning (depth + normal + segmentation), directly leveraging the user's free Blender buffers, and gives the best photorealism-per-training-effort. Weakness: diffusion sampling is stochastic, so per-view consistency must be **actively** enforced (shared initial noise per volume, consistency losses, optional cross-view attention).

**Instruction editing (InstructPix2Pix) / SDEdit — poor fit.** SDEdit "struggles to preserve identity and isolate individual objects," and the InstructPix2Pix family is documented to "invent or re-interpret an instruction" and cannot perform precise spatial control. Neither is appropriate when pixel-exact registration to fixed geometry is mandatory.

**Image-to-image diffusion (Palette-style).** Palette (Saharia et al., SIGGRAPH 2022) is a clean conditional diffusion baseline that "outperforms strong GAN and regression baselines" on colorization/inpainting/uncropping without auxiliary losses. Conceptually viable, but offers no pretrained photoreal prior and no built-in consistency mechanism, so it is strictly worse than ControlNet+SDXL for this task.

**Multi-view / 3D-aware diffusion (Zero-1-to-3, Zero123++, MVDream, SyncDreamer, Wonder3D).** These generate consistent view sets but are built for *novel-view synthesis from a single image*, not paired supervised translation of an existing registered 20-view set. They can inspire a cross-view-attention add-on but are not drop-in for paired data. DiffusionRenderer is the exception worth emulating: it is a paired G-buffer→image renderer with a video backbone whose temporal attention provides the multi-view coherence 3DGS needs.

### Conditioning recommendation (RQ3)

Condition on **depth + normals + segmentation-ID + flat RGB**, not RGB alone. The flat RGB already encodes per-organ identity (fixed palette) and silhouettes but says nothing about 3D surface orientation. Adding the geometry buffers contributes, per view, exactly the signals that determine view-consistent appearance: surface **normals** let the network place specular/coat lobes and bevel shading consistently as the camera orbits; **depth** supplies occlusion and aerial/fog-glow cues; the **segmentation-ID** map locks each tissue's material (roughness/IOR/SSS) to geometry rather than re-inferring it per view. Every one of the closest analogs (Intel EPE, DiffusionRenderer, FrameDiffuser) conditions on this same family of buffers and credits them with consistency. Since the user can export all of them from Blender at zero marginal cost, RGB-only is leaving the cheapest, highest-impact consistency gain on the table. Implement as a multi-condition ControlNet (sum control-branch outputs) or stack the buffers into the control input channels (FrameDiffuser's 9–10-channel concatenation is a direct template).

### Loss functions and training strategy (RQ4)

- **L1/L2 reconstruction** against the registered GT — primary fidelity term (you have exact pairs).
- **LPIPS / perceptual loss** — used by Intel's EPE and standard for texture realism; correlates better with perceived photorealism than pixel loss.
- **Feature-matching loss + multi-scale discriminator** (Pix2PixHD, λ=10) — stabilizes GAN training and sharpens detail if using the GAN path.
- **Fixed per-volume initial latent noise/seed** for the diffusion path — the cheapest cross-view consistency lever: share the starting noise across the 20 views of a volume so stochastic content cannot diverge per view.
- **Cross-view / video-consistency** — cross-frame attention as in Rerender-A-Video, FRESCO, and TokenFlow constrains shared key/value tokens across frames to hold style/texture/color coherent; effective but adds engineering complexity. Add only if needed.
- **Augmentation must be paired-identical** — any flip/crop/color-jitter must use identical parameters across the flat RGB, the GT, AND every geometry buffer, or registration breaks. Prefer geometric augmentations applied as one transform to the whole stack; avoid per-image color jitter on the GT (it would inject the very photometric inconsistency you are trying to remove).
- **Split train/val by volume, never by view**, to avoid leakage (the 20 views of a volume are near-duplicates).

### 3D-aware / integrated approaches (RQ5)

Rather than forcing full 3D consistency in 2D, the most robust pipeline for 3DGS specifically is **translate-per-view (with the consistency measures above) + absorb residual inconsistency in the splatting stage** via per-image appearance embeddings (Splatfacto-W / WildGaussians). This decouples a hard problem: the 2D translator gets lighting/texture approximately consistent, and the continuous appearance embeddings mop up the remainder *without affecting 3D geometry* (Splatfacto-W's explicit design property). For a master's thesis this is far more practical than training a bespoke multi-view diffusion model, and it provides a quantitative fallback (the documented +5.3 dB PSNR gain) if floaters appear. Training a custom multi-view/video-diffusion translator (DiffusionRenderer-style) is the higher-ceiling but research-grade option, justified only if the layered 2D approach fails.

### Practical fine-tuning config (RQ6)

- **Backbone:** SDXL (1024-native, matching your render resolution) with a trained ControlNet; or SD 1.5 + ControlNet if VRAM-limited (this is also FrameDiffuser's proven recipe for G-buffer→photoreal).
- **Resolution:** 1024×1024 native for SDXL (no down/upsampling of your renders).
- **LoRA vs full fine-tuning:** Train the **ControlNet branch** (optionally plus a rank-16–64 LoRA on the backbone). Full base fine-tuning of SDXL needs 24 GB+ and risks overfitting on 28.5k images; multiple sources note a full SDXL fine-tune "typically needs 24GB+ of VRAM." ControlNet-branch training is the correct granularity and is the documented robust regime for sub-50k datasets. Note one cautionary data point: at least one 2025 study found LoRA-only fine-tuning of a ControlNet branch underperformed full ControlNet-branch training on FID — so prefer training the full control branch rather than a thin LoRA on it.
- **VRAM:** SDXL ControlNet training realistically wants a 24 GB (or larger) GPU; SD 1.5 ControlNet trains comfortably on a single 16–24 GB card (FrameDiffuser used ~23 GB on an RTX 4090).
- **Steps:** On the order of tens of thousands of steps (the canonical ControlNet "fill50k" reference uses 50k images; DiffusionRenderer used 20,000 iterations; FrameDiffuser used ~80k total across its three stages). Monitor LPIPS/FID on held-out volumes.
- **Batch:** small (1–4) at 1024² with gradient accumulation (FrameDiffuser used batch 2 × accumulation 4 = effective 8).

### Medical/anatomical considerations (RQ7)

Paired GAN and diffusion image-to-image translation are heavily established in medical imaging — MedGAN (Armanious et al., Comput. Med. Imaging Graph. 2020), CycleGAN for MR↔CT and CBCT correction, and conditional diffusion models (Med-cDiff, MDPI Bioengineering 2023). The surgical-photorealism target does not change the core architecture choice, but two points sharpen it: (1) the realism objective here (Random-Walk subsurface scattering, wet specular tissue, coat lobes) is exactly the high-frequency, physically-plausible texture that a strong pretrained photoreal diffusion prior reproduces better than a from-scratch GAN — favoring the SDXL ControlNet primary; (2) anatomical correctness must be preserved, and geometry-buffer + segmentation-ID conditioning is the direct guard against the "hallucination" failure mode that pure-RGB or instruction-edit models exhibit (medical translation papers emphasize structure preservation / traceability for exactly this reason). Your data is derived from TotalSegmentator (Wasserthal et al., Radiology: AI 2023; 1,204 CT scans, 104 structures, Dice 0.943), so the label taxonomy is clean and stable — a good basis for ID-map conditioning.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Baseline (build first):** Pix2PixHD or SPADE conditioned on flat RGB only, at 1024². Deterministic, fast, stable; establishes an honest consistency floor. Benchmark by actually running the downstream 3DGS reconstruction and measuring held-out-view PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS plus a manual floater count. *Threshold to advance:* if 3DGS is floater-free but photorealism is unconvincing, move to Stage 2; if even this deterministic baseline floaters, your camera poses/registration export is the problem, not the network.

**Stage 2 — Primary system:** SDXL ControlNet conditioned on **depth + normals + segmentation-ID + flat RGB**, with a **fixed per-volume initial latent noise seed**, trained with L1 + LPIPS. This should deliver the best photorealism while geometry conditioning keeps appearance locked across the orbit. *Threshold:* compare 3DGS metrics and floater count against Stage 1.

**Stage 3 — Consistency hardening (apply as needed):** (a) Switch the 3DGS stage to **Splatfacto-W** with per-image appearance embeddings (expected order-of-magnitude-of-5-dB PSNR recovery on inconsistent inputs) — do this first, it is cheap and orthogonal. (b) If floaters persist, add cross-view attention / a video-consistency loss (Rerender/FRESCO/TokenFlow-style) to the translator.

**Escalation threshold:** If Stage-2 outputs + appearance embeddings still produce floaters/blur in 3DGS, escalate to a **video-diffusion backbone that attends across views jointly** (DiffusionRenderer/SVD-style), accepting the larger compute (multi-A100, 512² clips) and engineering cost. Conversely, if photorealism is adequate but diffusion training is unstable or compute is the binding constraint, fall back to the deterministic **SPADE/Pix2PixHD + geometry buffers** path, which is consistent by construction.

## Caveats
- **3DGS will expose ANY cross-view inconsistency** — this is the dominant risk and the reason geometry conditioning, deterministic/shared-noise generation, and appearance embeddings are all recommended *together* rather than singly. No single trick is sufficient.
- **Multi-view diffusion methods (Zero123++, MVDream, SyncDreamer, Wonder3D) target novel-view synthesis from sparse input, not paired translation;** adapting them to paired supervision is research-grade effort and not advised as a first move.
- **Diffusion outputs at 1024² with a fixed seed are only approximately consistent;** the appearance-embedding safety net (Splatfacto-W/WildGaussians) is effectively mandatory if truly floater-free 3DGS is the bar.
- **Some 2025 sources are very recent** (DiffusionRenderer CVPR 2025; FrameDiffuser arXiv Dec 2025). DiffusionRenderer's exact forward-rendering benchmark numbers should be confirmed against the CVPR PDF Table 1 if used quantitatively; the architecture details (SVD backbone, `{n,d,a,r,m,E}` conditioning, 512² / 20k-iter training) are confirmed from the paper and project page.
- **FrameDiffuser's autoregressive consistency mechanism is causal/sequential**, designed for interactive frame streaming; for a fixed 20-view 3DGS capture, DiffusionRenderer's joint multi-frame attention is the more apt consistency model, while FrameDiffuser's SD1.5+ControlNet 9-channel G-buffer recipe is the best lightweight *implementation template* to copy.
